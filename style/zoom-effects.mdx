To add a zoom in effect in Daydream, ask the AI something like "punch in when I say 'the price'" or "slow push-in over my intro". It animates the clip with keyframes, using a springy ease that moves like a real camera settling.

## Ask for a zoom or move

> Punch in when I say "the price", then back out after the sentence.

> Slow push-in over the first 5 seconds.

> Zoom in on the top left of the screen when I click the Save button.

> Add a subtle zoom to every B-roll shot.

> Slide the title in from the left and fade it out at the end.

A **punch-in** is a quick zoom that snaps in and settles, good for emphasis in talking-head videos. A **push-in** is slow and steady, good for drawing attention over a few seconds. If you don't say which, the AI picks based on how you describe it.

It can zoom on a specific spot, like your face, a product or part of a screen recording, and keep that spot steady while it zooms.

## Add motion blur

For a smoother, more cinematic move, ask for motion blur:

> Add motion blur to the zooms.

Motion blur smears the picture along the direction of movement, the way a real camera shutter does, so fast zooms look less digital.

## Fades and crossfades

The same keyframes control opacity, so the AI can fade clips in and out:

> Fade in from black at the start and fade out at the end.

> Crossfade between these two clips.

There's no transitions menu in Daydream. For anything beyond fades, see [custom effects and transitions](/style/custom-effects-and-transitions).

## Keyframes by hand

Select a clip and open **Properties**. In **Transform**, each value (position, scale, rotation and opacity) has a diamond button that adds a keyframe at the playhead. Move the playhead, change the value, and Daydream animates between the two.

Keyframes the AI adds show up the same way, so you can adjust their timing or values after it's done.

Volume can be keyframed too, which is how fades and dips in audio work. See [mix your sound](/edit/mix-sound).

## Limits

- Color settings can't be keyframed.
- Keyframes move with the clip. If you trim the start of a clip, check that its keyframes still land where you want.
