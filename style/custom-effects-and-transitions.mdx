If a look isn't in Daydream's [built-in effects](/style/effects), describe it and the AI writes a custom effect for your project. That covers looks like CRT scanlines, halftone, chromatic aberration or pixel art, transitions between clips, effects that only touch the background or only the person, and effects that pulse with your music.

## Ask for a look

> Make this clip look like an old CRT TV.

> Add a halftone effect, like a comic book.

> Give the intro a chromatic aberration glitch that gets stronger toward the end.

> Turn the B-roll into pixel art.

{/* Visual: loops of two or three custom looks on the same clip */}

The AI writes the effect, checks that it runs smoothly on your computer, and applies it. If it's too heavy for smooth playback, the AI makes a lighter version. Then it looks at a frame of the result before telling you it's done.

## Adjust it

Custom effects come with settings the AI can tune, like strength, color or size:

> Make the scanlines thinner and less dark.

> Change the halftone dots to pink.

You can also ask to change how it works ("make it flicker less") and the AI edits the effect. Every clip using it updates at once, and it's one undo step.

## Only the background, or only the person

Custom effects can use the same cutout as [background blur](/style/background-and-text-behind), so they can affect one part of the frame:

> Add grain only to the background, not to me.

> Wash the background in blue and keep me in normal color.

The first time an effect like this runs on a clip, Daydream analyzes the clip to find you, which takes about 2 minutes per minute of footage.

## Transitions

Daydream doesn't have a transitions menu, but the AI can make any transition you can describe:

> Add a whip-pan transition between these two clips.

> Make an ink-bleed transition into the B-roll.

> Use a glitch transition between every cut in the montage.

To build a transition, the AI overlaps the next clip with the current one on the track above and blends between them. For a plain fade, it uses opacity instead, as described in [add zooms and camera moves](/style/zoom-effects#fades-and-crossfades).

## Effects that move with the music

Custom effects can react to the song on your timeline: hitting on every beat, or growing with how loud the music is.

> Add a zoom punch on every beat.

> Make the background glow pulse with the music.

> Flash and shake the frame on the drop.

{/* Visual: loop with sound of an effect pulsing on the beat */}

See [edit to the beat](/edit/edit-to-the-beat) for how Daydream finds the beats.

## Borrow from Paper Shaders

[Paper Shaders](https://shaders.paper.design) has a gallery of effects. Paste the link to one you like into the chat and ask the AI to use it on your clip. It adapts the effect for video.

## Good to know

- Custom effects belong to the project they were made in. To use one in another project, ask the AI to make it again, or save the steps in a [recipe](/automate/recipes).
- If your computer's graphics restart, Daydream may switch off a custom effect to stay stable. Ask the AI to turn it back on.
- MP4 exports include custom effects. When you send your edit to another editor, effects on clips are rendered into new video files. Transitions and beat effects go on a new track above the original, which stays underneath. Effects over the whole frame aren't included in those handoffs.
