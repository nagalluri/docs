To blur or remove your background in Daydream, ask the AI: "blur my background" or "remove my background and put this image behind me". Daydream cuts you out of every frame on your own computer, follows you as you move, and applies the effect only where it should. No green screen needed.

## What you can ask for

| Ask for | What you get |
|---|---|
| "Blur my background" | Portrait-mode blur. You stay sharp and everything else blurs, like a real lens. |
| "Remove my background" | Only you remain, with the tracks below showing through. |
| "Replace my background with beach.mp4" | You over the image or video you name. |
| "Put me on a solid blue background" | You over a flat color. |
| "Make the background black and white" | Color pop: you stay in color, the rest goes gray. |
| "Outline me" | A sticker-style stroke or soft glow around you. |
| "Put the title behind me" | Text behind your head and shoulders, in front of the background. |

## Text behind you

> Put a big "HOW I SAVE $1,000 A MONTH" behind me.

> Put the captions behind me.

The AI places the text on a track above your footage and tucks it behind you. It works best with big, bold, condensed type that your head clearly overlaps. Your head hiding part of a word is the look, so the AI keeps each word whole and in one line rather than splitting it around you. If too much is hidden to read, ask for it smaller or higher.

## How long it takes

The first time you use one of these effects on a clip, Daydream analyzes it to find you in every frame. On a recent Mac, that takes about 2 minutes per minute of footage. The clip shows its progress on the timeline, and the AI waits for it to finish before telling you it's done.

Until analysis reaches a frame, that frame shows without the effect. Analysis pauses while you play the video or export, then picks up again. On computers whose graphics chip can't run it, Daydream uses the processor instead, which is slower.

Once a clip is analyzed, switching between these effects or changing them is instant.

## Choose who or what stays in front

By default, the cutout keeps every person in the frame. Tell the AI if you want something different:

> Only keep the person on the left.

> Keep the mic and the ring light in front of me too.

> Keep the product I'm holding.

Changing what's kept means analyzing the clip again.

## Fix a cutout that looks wrong

If an edge isn't right, describe what you see:

> My hand disappears when I wave.

> The cutout spills onto the desk.

> There's a halo around my hair.

The AI zooms in on the problem, then fixes it: removing things that shouldn't be there, adding things that should, or tightening or softening the edge. It checks the same frames again before telling you it's fixed.

## Limits

- These effects work on video clips, and the cutout is most accurate on people.
- Analysis runs on your computer, so long clips take a while. Trim first if you only need part of a clip.

## In exports

MP4 exports include everything as you see it. When you send your edit to Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro or CapCut, cutouts and background effects come across as rendered video when **Keep color grade & effects** is on (the default), with transparency where the background was removed.
