Download Daydream from [daydreamvideo.com/download](https://www.daydreamvideo.com/download), install it like any other app, and sign in. It works on Mac and Windows and keeps itself up to date.

## System requirements

| | Mac | Windows |
|---|---|---|
| Computer | Apple silicon, or an Intel Mac from 2016 or later | 64-bit PC (x64). Windows on ARM isn't supported yet. |
| Operating system | macOS 13 Ventura or later | Windows 10 or 11 |
| Graphics | Built in | A graphics card or chip with up-to-date drivers |
| Disk space | Room for your footage, plus a few GB for editing copies and caches | Same |

Daydream needs a graphics chip that supports WebGL 2, which every Mac and PC from the last several years has. On an older Intel Mac without it, Daydream shows a notice saying your device may be unsupported.

## Install on a Mac

1. Download the Mac version. The site picks the right build for Apple silicon or Intel.
2. Open the downloaded file and drag **Daydream** into **Applications**.
3. Open Daydream from Applications. If you open it from somewhere else, like your Downloads folder, Daydream offers to move itself to Applications. Say yes, because updates only install from there.

## Install on Windows

1. Download the Windows installer.
2. Run it and follow the steps. Daydream opens when it's done.

## Sign in

When Daydream opens, sign in with **Continue with Google** or with your email and password. If you don't have an account yet, click **Create an account**, which opens the sign-up page in your browser.

After you sign in, Daydream asks you to connect Claude or ChatGPT. That takes a minute or two, and [connect Claude or ChatGPT](/connect-claude-chatgpt) walks through it.

## Updates

Daydream checks for updates in the background. When one is ready, you'll see **New Update Available**. Click **Restart & Update** to install it, or **Not now** to keep working. You can also check yourself from the **Daydream** menu with **Check for Updates**. If an export is running, Daydream waits for it to finish before restarting.

## Permission prompts on Mac

While you work, macOS may ask whether Daydream can use your camera, microphone, photos or other apps. These prompts come from commands the AI runs, not from Daydream itself. Allow them if you asked for something that needs them, and deny them if they're unexpected. Editing works either way.
